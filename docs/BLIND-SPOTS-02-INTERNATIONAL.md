# Blind Spots 02 — International / Global-South Coverage

**Research date:** 2026-11-17 · **Scope:** gap analysis of `PLAN.md` and `USE-CASES-EXPANSION-V2.md` for non-US / international coverage.
**Method:** grep + targeted reads of both docs (no full-file reads), then 2025–2026 primary-source research (EU Commission, EDPB, ICO, MSIT Korea, NIST, ISO, ITU, PIB India, Safaricom, BCB Brazil, national law texts).

## 1. Executive summary

Both source docs are **overwhelmingly US-centric**. `PLAN.md` §29 (Human Problems Atlas), §25 (Use-Case Library) and the whole of `USE-CASES-EXPANSION-V2.md` are built on US institutions and law: FCRA, FDCPA, HIPAA, IRS/Form 990/TEOS, SSA/SSDI/SSI, Medicaid/Medicare, FAFSA, USCIS, state portals, US banks, US credit bureaus, and US telephony (FCC/TCPA). The one deliberate international section, `USE-CASES-EXPANSION-V2.md` §2.17 *Cross-border citizens & movers*, is only 12 rows (jure sanguinis, consular slots, EU/UK VAT refunds, multi-country pensions, S1/EHIC/NHSBSA, NRI/OCI, German Anmeldung, EC261). `PLAN.md` §23.4 *Standards posture table* covers only agent-protocol standards (MCP, A2A, Skills, Plugins, AGENTS.md, WebMCP, agent identity, x402, sandboxes) — no regulatory or management-system standards.

**Confirmed absent (verified by grep, not inferred):**

| Topic | Grep result across both docs |
|---|---|
| GDPR, EU AI Act, DSA, Data Act, GPAI, "Europe" | **0 matches** |
| ISO/IEC 42001, ISO 27001, NIST AI RMF, ITU, AI RMF | **0 substantive matches** |
| Cross-border data transfer, data residency, localization, i18n, multilingual, non-English | **0 matches** |
| India Stack, Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, Pix, M-Pesa, SEPA, mobile money, Africa, Japan, Korea, China, Canada (as jurisdiction), Brazil | **0 matches** (one NRI/OCI row in §2.17; "India" false positives from "pixels") |
| Time-zone / DST / non-Gregorian calendar handling | **0 matches** (only meeting-calendar negotiation) |

The docs' payment seam is explicitly **x402 / AP2 / Mastercard Agent Pay only** (`PLAN.md` L772; `USE-CASES-EXPANSION-V2.md` source register L432) — no UPI, Pix, SEPA Instant, FedNow or mobile money. The permissions engine and vault are Windows/DPAPI-only (`PLAN.md` §6.6, §11). There is no concept of "which jurisdiction is the user in," no data-residency boundary, and no locale model beyond a11y-tree selectors.

**Why this is a real product risk, not a checklist omission (as of 2026-11-17):**

- The **EU AI Act is now in its enforcement era**: generally applicable since **2 Aug 2026**, with AI Office + member-state authorities enforcing since that date; GPAI duties since 2 Aug 2025; Article 50 transparency since Aug 2026. The **AI Omnibus** (proposed 19 Nov 2025, political agreement 7 May 2026, **in force 27 Jul 2026**) pushed Annex III high-risk obligations to **2 Dec 2027** and Annex I embedded-product high-risk to **2 Aug 2028** — but *simultaneously widened AI Office oversight* to AI systems built on GPAI and embedded in large platforms/search engines. A locally-run general-purpose computer agent is squarely in the "GPAI-based system" conversation ([EC AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), updated 19 Oct 2026; [AI Omnibus enters into force](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force), 27 Jul 2026).
- **GDPR/UK GDPR automated-decision rules now bite agent pipelines**: GDPR Art 22 remains the baseline; the UK's Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 s.80 **replaced Art 22 UK GDPR on 5 Feb 2026** with a more permissive but safeguard-heavy regime; EDPB's 2026 DPIA consultation feedback calls persisted agent tool-call logs and inferences a *defining compliance risk for agentic AI*.
- **The Global South is where the volume is**: UPI processed ~**228 bn transactions in 2025** (~49% of global fast-payment volume); Pix ~**80 bn / R$35 tn in 2025** and ~54.7% of Brazilian retail payments; Aadhaar covers ~**1.4 bn residents**; Africa moved roughly **$1.4 tn** through mobile money in 2025. None of these appear in the docs, so entire classes of agent work (paying, proving identity, retrieving documents, consenting to data access) are unmodelled.
- **Standards are a procurement/market-access gate**: ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable AI management-system standard; the first AI-Act-supporting harmonised standard was published in the Official Journal on **19 Oct 2026**. A product with no answer to "are you ISO 27001 / 42001 aligned, do you map to NIST AI RMF, can you produce a conformity file" cannot enter EU enterprise or public procurement — and the docs' §23.4 table would suggest to a reader that standards posture is *only* protocol standards.

**Bottom line:** before any EU/UK/India/APAC/Africa launch, the harness needs (a) a jurisdiction dimension in its policy engine, (b) a regulatory/compliance seam (AI Act classification + GDPR/DUAA ADM + DPIA + cross-border transfer gates), (c) payment and identity adapters for non-card rails, (d) a locale/time model, and (e) an international source registry for the existing rule-change-alarm primitive.

## 4. Top 5 in depth

### 4.1 EU AI Act — the single most urgent, and the docs are stale on the dates (P0)

**What changed.** The AI Act (Reg (EU) 2024/1689) entered into force 1 Aug 2024 and became **generally applicable on 2 Aug 2026**. The timeline is staged: prohibitions 1–8 + AI literacy from 2 Feb 2025; governance and **GPAI obligations from 2 Aug 2025**; AI Office enforcement powers over GPAI from 2 Aug 2026; Article 50 transparency from Aug 2026. Then the **AI Omnibus** — proposed by the Commission on 19 Nov 2025, political agreement 7 May 2026, **in force 27 Jul 2026** — deferred **Annex III high-risk** rules to **2 Dec 2027** and **Annex I** (AI embedded in products) to **2 Aug 2028**, while *expanding* the AI Office's oversight to systems built on GPAI models and embedded in large online platforms/search engines, and adding a ninth prohibition (nudification/CSAM) effective Dec 2026. The first harmonised standard supporting the AI Act appeared in the Official Journal on **19 Oct 2026**. ([EC AI Act page, updated 19 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); [AI Omnibus enters into force, 27 Jul 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force))

**Why the harness is exposed.** The product is a general-purpose agent that "operates your entire computer," including drafting/publishing content (Art 50 transparency), operating in employment/credit/essential-services/migration contexts if the user asks it to process HR, loan, benefits or visa portals (Annex III high-risk domains), and is itself a GPAI-based system. The docs' §23.4 standards table would lead a reader to believe the compliance surface is MCP/A2A/x402 only.

**What to add (concrete).**
1. `agent.toml` fields: `jurisdiction`, `ai_act.role` (provider/deployer), `deployment_context`.
2. A risk-classification function with **date-versioned** rule data (the Omnibus means the correct answer differs before/after 27 Jul 2026 and 2 Dec 2027).
3. Automatic Art 50 disclosure on agent-generated text/image/audio — reuse the existing draft-first outbound path and approval card, so disclosure is *unskippable*.
4. GPAI Code of Practice + training-content-summary artifacts as ledger receipts.
5. A self-audit command (`harness compliance ai-act --as-of 2026-08-03`) that produces a conformity file for a given date.

### 4.2 GDPR / UK DUAA — agentic "automated decisions" and the per-step trigger (P0)

**What changed.** GDPR Art 22 still grants a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. The EDPB's Apr-2025 LLM privacy paper clarifies when a DPIA (Art 35) and an AI Act FRIA are required; EDPB's 2026 DPIA consultation feedback identifies persisted agent tool-call logs, transit data and inferred facts as a defining agentic-AI compliance risk. In the UK, **s.80 of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 came into force 5 Feb 2026**, replacing Art 22 UK GDPR with a more permissive regime (any lawful basis, with mandatory safeguards) — and ICO draft guidance explicitly notes it sets the trigger at the *decision*, not at each step of an agentic pipeline. ([EDPB LLM paper](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2025-04/ai-privacy-risks-and-mitigations-in-llms.pdf); [UK gov DUAA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-use-and-access-act-2025-data-protection-and-privacy-changes); [Hogan Lovells, 5 Feb 2026](https://hlc.com/en/publications/ai-and-automated-decisionmaking-in-the-uk-part-i-the-new-rules-and-regulatory-guidance); [ICO ADM guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/rights-related-to-automated-decision-making-including-profiling))

**Why the harness is exposed.** Its four behavior levels (`auto / auto-if-preapproved / confirm / handoff`) are a *UX* control, not a legal basis or a durable record of meaningful human involvement. A multi-step agent action that ends in an automated adverse outcome (a rejected application, a cancelled service) is exactly the fact pattern. And the audit chain currently protects *the system's* integrity, not a data subject's rights.

**What to add.** Legal-basis + ADM register per action class; a signed "human review" proof artifact attached to every `confirm`/`handoff` decision; generated DPIA/FRIA documents in the plan folder; a data-subject-rights workflow that can erase the fact tier, session DB, tool-call logs and persisted inferences; and an explicit single-decision vs multi-step classification so the per-step trigger gap is not silently crossed.

### 4.3 Global payment rails — the seam exists, the rails don't (P1)

UPI reached ~228 bn transactions in 2025 and accounts for ~49% of global fast-payment volume; Pix processed ~80 bn transactions worth R$35 tn in 2025 and represented 54.7% of Brazilian retail payments in H2 2025; SEPA Instant/Faster Payments/FedNow complete the A2A picture. None are in the docs, which name only x402/AP2. ([PIB/IMF UPI](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2200569&lang=2&reg=48); [Pix statistics, BCB](https://www.bcb.gov.br/en/financialstability/pixstatistics))

The correct move is not "add a payments feature" but **generalise the existing seam**: a `PaymentRail` interface capturing identifier scheme (VPA / Pix key / IBAN / card / crypto address), currency precision, confirmation irrevocability (A2A rails have no chargeback), dispute/refund semantics, and jurisdiction. Keep the non-negotiable "never auto-pay" rule but bind it per rail — irreversible instant rails deserve the strictest tier. This is a contained change because §23.4 already declares the seam.

### 4.4 India Stack + DPDP Rules 2025 — consent as a first-class, revocable object (P1)

Aadhaar covers ~1.4 bn residents with 2,707 crore authentication transactions in FY2024-25; India Stack layers Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker and Account Aggregator behind open APIs; the **DPDP Rules 2025** were notified in Nov 2025 with an 18-month phased rollout (Consent Manager provisions ~Nov 2026, the rest ~May 2027). ([India Stack](https://indiastack.org); [PIB stats](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2235812&lang=2&reg=3); [DPDP timeline](https://dpdp.lkslaw.com/compliance/timelines))

India is the largest single market for a personal computer agent, and uniquely, the state has already built the identity/document/payment/consent APIs the agent would need. The conceptual bridge to the existing design is strong: the Account Aggregator consent object maps onto the **Delegation Ledger + Consent-Receipt Pairing**, and DigiLocker documents map onto the **Relay Baton**. The harness should add a `consent_object` type (fiduciary, purpose, scope, time-box, revocable) and emit consent receipts in the user's own inspectable store — exactly the "file-first, user-inspectable" thesis already in the docs.

### 4.5 International standards — make "verifiable" portable (P1)

The docs' differentiation is "boring, verifiable, local." Today that verifiability is expressed only as in-repo artifacts (hash-chained audit, receipts, ID card). Adding ISO/IEC 42001 (AIMS) alignment, ISO 27001 controls for the vault/egress, NIST AI RMF tags (`GOVERN/MAP/MEASURE/MANAGE`) on every control, and tracking the ITU/ISO standardisation feed (700+ standards indexed; first AI-Act harmonised standard in OJ Oct 2026) turns an internal property into a **customer-legible, auditable** one. Concretely: a `controls-mapping.yaml` that maps each harness safeguard to ISO 42001 clause, NIST AI RMF function and AI Act article — and an export that a procurement or conformity assessor can read. ([ISO 42001](https://iso.org/standard/42001); [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework); [ITU standards report](https://itu.int/dms_pub/itu-t/opb/ai4g/T-AI4G-AI4GOOD-2025-4-PDF-E.pdf))
