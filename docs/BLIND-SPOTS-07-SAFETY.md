## 1. Executive summary

The plan's safety story is unusually strong on **incidents, egress control, and multi-agent trust choreography** (§24, §31), and it already pre-empts several things a naive audit would call gaps — variance-aware evaluation, multi-CaMeL, Reader/Actor, MCP advisories, and agent-identity headers are all present (§2). What is missing is a different layer: **the machinery that makes the harness's own controls provably correct, observable, and adaptive without letting the model rewrite them.**

Fourteen substantive gaps surfaced. The five most consequential:

1. **The plan's headline prompt-injection defense is an in-band pattern class that the 2025–2026 literature has broken under adaptive attack.** StruQ, SecAlign/Meta-SecAlign, Spotlighting, instruction hierarchy and detector-based guards fall to >90% ASR when the attacker moves second; the surviving boundary is *capability isolation* (CaMeL/FIDES/Progent/FORGE) — a family the plan only touches via multi-CaMeL (§31). §6.7/§24.3 must be reframed from "Reader/Actor is the defense" to "Reader/Actor is a utility filter; the deterministic capability boundary is the defense."
2. **No formal or temporal guarantee over the policy engine.** §6's first-match-wins rule engine is deterministic but unverified: 2026 runtime-verification work (FORGE, ActGov, Vigil, VEPA, AgentSpec) shows how to add SMT-checked, trace-level, cross-agent policies at millisecond overhead and >95% recall — including a published method for **evolving guardrails from deployment feedback without letting the model activate its own patches** (VEPA), which is precisely the mechanism behind §24.4's "model can never dismiss its own gate."
3. **The sandbox ladder has no kernel boundary.** §6.4 calls L4 Docker "NOT a security boundary" and §26.2 puts MXC microVMs "on the roadmap," while gVisor, Kata and Firecracker have become commodity, production-proven boundaries (CNCF acceptance, guest-escape advisories, 125 ms boots). A microVM holding a write token and open egress is still a breach — the missing piece is **credential exclusion + kernel boundary together**, not another process tier.
4. **Our own benchmarks are an unguarded attack surface.** §22 trusts published scores and §24.5 #9 trusts reruns; neither addresses contamination or reward hacking. BenchJack got near-perfect scores on 9/10 agent benchmarks without solving a task; search-time contamination inflates deep-research scores; SWE-bench Verified was retired by its own co-creator. Our eval discipline needs an adversarial harness audit before any number is quoted.
5. **Activation-level deception monitoring is now deployable on local models.** §31.5's private-belief audit reads *behavior*; 2026 probes read *internal states* — 98.8% AUC on SHADE-Arena, 90.4% AUROC up to seven steps **before** deceptive action, and steering that converts 56% of deceptive trajectories to honest ones. This is a natural fit for the plan's small-local-model bet (§22.2), with strict false-positive budgets and sequential detection.

Also material: conformal/abstention for autonomy (§1 table #5; no finite-sample deferral guarantee anywhere in the plan), OpenTelemetry GenAI telemetry (no wire format despite "variance-aware telemetry"), RAG/retrieval as the decisive compromise step for the §25.6 second-brain, EU AI Act Art. 55/9/15 evidence duties, agentic energy/water/carbon accounting, SPIFFE-style agentic IAM, and late-2026 protocol deltas (A2A v1.1 RC, AP2 v0.2, x402 V2).

## 4. Top 5 in depth

### 4.1 B-01 — The in-band defense graveyard, and what survives

**What the plan says.** §6.7 gives a Reader/Actor split (cheap model digests pages; Actor never sees raw DOM), hidden-text flagging, and a CI injection corpus. §24.3 calls dual-runner Reader/Actor "the strongest published defense pattern" with SA-CUA's ASR→0, and §24.4 builds the page-chained-injection defense on it. §31.10 adds multi-CaMeL's provenance-preserving channels (ASR 12.9%→0.0%). That is a coherent 2025-era posture.

**What changed.** The 2025–26 literature separates defenses into *in-band* (inside the model/channel: classifiers, instruction hierarchy, spotlighting, adversarial fine-tuning) and *out-of-band* (deterministic policy outside the model: CaMeL, FIDES, Progent, RTBAS, FORGE). The in-band column has been systematically broken:

- arXiv:2505.15738 raises StruQ from near-zero to **100% ASR** and SecAlign to **96%** with checkpoint-based adaptive GCG; universal suffixes optimized on a surrogate transfer to a separately released defended model.
- arXiv:2606.26479 reports **twelve published defenses above 90%** under adaptive attack — spotlighting and sandwiching above 95%, PromptGuard/Model Armor above 90%, MetaSecAlign 96%; detector Attention Tracker has FNR 0.69 on MMLU-PI and a trivial separator-token attack drives it to 1.00.
- arXiv:2608.27092 tests SecAlign as the decision model of a real tool-calling agent: channel separation (38.8%) and SecAlign (32.5%) leave the exfiltration "framing gap" wide open, while payload-blind capability isolation (destination allowlist, planner/reader split) reaches **0%**.
- USENIX Security 2026 SoK lists all these defenses by paradigm and notes the field's own reporting failure: static benchmarks measured resistance to a known attack set and were read as resistance to attackers.

**Why it changes our design.** None of this invalidates Reader/Actor — SA-CUA is a *structural* split of the observation channel, and §24.4's sequence-aware scanning is sensible. It invalidates treating any *model-reading* component (classifier, fine-tuned guard, "ignored not refused" prompt) as the wall. §24.3's own lesson — "classifier ≠ boundary; egress control is the real wall" — is correct, but §6.7 still reads like the digest is the defense. The honest architecture is: **capability labels + deterministic action gate + egress allowlist are the boundary; the digest is a utility/context-hygiene layer.** The plan already has most of the pieces (§24.5 #1, #3, #4, #6); it needs the explicit claim and the adaptive test corpus so a future contributor cannot "improve" security by swapping in SecAlign.

**Acceptance criteria.** (1) An injection corpus section with adaptive attacks (GCG variants, canary-blind entanglement, phantom continuation, architecture-aware rewrites) runs in CI, not just static page variants. (2) A documented invariant: no model-produced text can add a capability, relax a rule, or change a destination allowlist. (3) Security claims in docs cite the deterministic boundary, never a static ASR number.

### 4.2 B-02/B-03 — Verified shields and guards that cannot rewrite themselves

**What the plan says.** §6.1's pipeline is `canonicalize → rule engine → tier → sandbox → execute → audit log`; resolution is first-match-wins across global/project/session/interactive scopes. It is careful about canonicalization (PATH hijacks, 8.3 names, junctions, compound commands) but each decision is local to one call. §24.4 records that Anthropic's classifier "blocked cleanup of its own compromise" and states the invariant "guard refactored out of band; model can never dismiss its own gate." §31.10 adds that emergent norms can never change a tier. There is no described mechanism for either.

**What 2026 offers.**

- **FORGE** (arXiv:2602.16708, v4 Oct 2026): declarative policies over execution history with recursive queries, causal dependencies and cross-agent tracking; candidate actions are evaluated *before* effect; under complete mediation + faithful observation it gives a formal guarantee that policy-violating actions cannot execute. Its capability matrix shows Progent, NeMo Guardrails, AgentSpec, ShieldAgent all lack recursive queries, causal deps and multi-agent support.
- **Vigil** (arXiv:2606.26524): finite-trace temporal policy language compiled to SMT; on 152 labeled runs it detects multi-step violations that bypass AgentSpec/Progent with **95.8% recall at 89.6% precision**; on 216 real skill-bundle executions it surfaced 34 confirmed violations, including a composition-level defect in NVIDIA's skill ecosystem that was acknowledged. This is directly analogous to our SKILL.md/plugin trust model (§8, §23.4).
- **ActGov** (arXiv:2609.24446): LLM-proposed layered policies validated by **Z3** before deployment; AgentDojo/AgentDyn results.
- **VEPA** (arXiv:2610.12813): the exact answer to "can guards learn without self-modification" — LLM proposes policy patches from deployment feedback, a deterministic verifier accepts only structurally valid, conflict-free policies, and an evidence-aware authority layer activates a rule only with enough supporting evidence. Holds under mixed streams, corrupted feedback and longer horizons.
- **Survey** (arXiv:2608.14590): 38 studies; runtime monitoring is the only paradigm with production deployment (AgentSpec >90% unsafe-execution prevention, millisecond-per-rule overhead) but only one-step/short-horizon guarantees; the "verifier tax" — intercepting individually unsafe actions does not reliably produce safe task completion — is the field's most consequential finding. Design for that: a monitor reduces risk, it does not create safety.
- **Caveat from formal RV** (MFOTL/MonPoly study, 2026): generic obligations flagged 70–72% of attacks but also fired on 29.3% of benign runs because traces rarely recorded approvals or timestamps; a planted provenance line defeated a naive check on 94–99% of runs, and *binding provenance to the lookup that produced it* closed the evasion at no cost. **Our trace schema must record approvals, timestamps and lookup-bound provenance or the monitor will be noisy and bypassable.**

**Concrete addition.** Add a `policy_monitor` component alongside the rule engine:

1. A small DSL for temporal obligations over the hash-chained trace: `never(send, after(vault.get, within=task))`, `requires(send, verifier_verdict(ok))`, `at_most_once(irreversible_op, key=hash)`, `no_tier_change_by(actor=model)`.
2. `policy.toml` validated at load (and at every proposed change) with Z3: detect conflicts, unreachable denies, and scopes broader than the project root.
3. Mutations follow VEPA's three phases — propose (LLM or human) → verify (deterministic) → activate (evidence quorum + two-key signoff for downgrades). Every version hash-chained.
4. Start in **shadow mode** (log violations, measure FP rate); promote to hard gate only after a measured FP budget is met. The verifier-tax finding says a monitor is a risk reducer, not a completion guarantee.

**Acceptance criteria:** 10 standing obligations expressed in the DSL; zero policy changes reachable from model output; shadow-mode FP rate <2% on the 20–50 task eval suite (§13) before hard-gating; a regression test reproducing the Anthropic "blocked its own cleanup" scenario.

### 4.3 B-07 — Put a kernel boundary in the ladder, and keep credentials out

**What the plan says.** §6.4's L0 restricted token → L1 AppContainer → L2 Windows Sandbox → L3 Hyper-V VM → L4 WSL2/Docker, with the explicit note that L4 is "convenience isolation, NOT a security boundary." §26.2 says MXC has "micro-VMs on the roadmap." §30.3 lists Firecracker microVMs as "EASY ★ (buy, don't build)". So the *awareness* exists; what's missing is any integration into the default execution path or a credential model for it.

**What 2026 evidence says.** The incident record in §24 (Medicare sandbox escape, JFrog zero-day → Modal C2, Snowflake process-substitution escape) is precisely kernel/credential-class. The 2026 sandbox landscape has matured into three credible boundaries: gVisor (userspace Sentry + seccomp; ~200–600 ms, 18–50 MB; CNCF voted to accept 2026-09-28; runs under GKE Sandbox, Modal, Cloud Run first-gen), Kata Containers (guest kernel in a microVM behind KVM; 4.3.0+ required after two 2026-10-21 advisories including a Dragonball guest-to-host escape), and Firecracker (~125 ms boot, <5 MiB, ~50k-line Rust VMM, E2B/Lambda/Fly.io/Vercel). MXC itself reached **GA on 7 Oct 2026 (v1.0.1, 17 Oct)** — newer than §26.2's description.

The most useful 2026 finding is negative: a well-isolated VM holding a write-scoped repo token and open internet **is still a breach**, and `devcontainer.json`/`.git/hooks` writes execute on the host despite the container. The "lethal trifecta" (private data + untrusted content + external communication) is closed by removing one leg — which is exactly what §6.5 (deny egress), §6.6 (vault placeholders, secrets never in context) and §24.5 #1/#7 already do. The boundary and the credential story must be designed together.

**Concrete addition.** Extend §6.4 with two tiers and one rule:

- **L3.5 gVisor / OCI `runsc`** for container-native, high-density, untrusted-but-syscall-predictable workloads (analysis scripts, test execution).
- **L5 microVM (Firecracker or Kata ≥4.3.0)** for untrusted repos, third-party skills/plugins, and any autonomous long-horizon run. GPU work → Kata with VFIO; no GPU + tight cold start → Firecracker with snapshot restore.
- **Rule:** no long-lived credential ever exists inside L3.5/L5. Secrets arrive as §6.6 placeholders and are substituted by the executor outside the sandbox; egress deny-by-default applies inside the guest; approval prompts are explicitly *not* counted as a containment control.

**Acceptance criteria:** malicious-repo fixture from §13 (evil `package.json` lifecycle script, `.git/hooks` write) executes inside L5 and cannot touch host files; a canary credential placed in the VM is unreadable by the agent; measured cold-start + memory overhead published per tier.

### 4.4 B-09 — Audit the benchmarks before we design against them

**What the plan says.** §22.1 lists leaderboard numbers and draws design conclusions ("single-window tasks nearly solved"; OSWorld 2.0 binary 20.6%); §26.4 selects OSWorld 2.0 long-horizon subsets as our eval target; §24.5 #9 tracks rerun variance. There is no mention of contamination, grader integrity, or reward hacking against our own evals.

**What the evidence says.** BenchJack (arXiv:2605.12673) generated near-perfect scores on 9 of 10 popular agent benchmarks without solving any task — a nine-line `conftest.py` PyTest hook forces 100% SWE-bench Verified; WebArena's gold answers sit in a JSON file on the agent-accessible filesystem. It found 219 distinct flaws in eight classes. arXiv:2608.29463 adds a **Type 5 "acquired" contamination** category specific to agents: answers obtained at *evaluation time* from the environment, git history, or web retrieval — which bypasses training decontamination entirely. Search-Time Contamination (arXiv:2606.05241) inflates deep-research scores by up to 4% (60% answer leakage for one agent). SWE-bench Verified's own co-creator stopped reporting it in Feb 2026 after 59.4% of audited problems had tests that reject correct solutions. OSWorld grader drift broke 13/46 Chrome tasks and understated one agent by 28 points. METR found test-passing runs on real repos produced no mergeable PRs. arXiv:2605.21404's 5-field audit schema (benchmark identity, harness spec, inference settings, cost, failure breakdown) shows that even reproducibility is under-specified.

**Why it matters for us.** Our §31.11 TeamWork-Bench will become the number the team optimizes. If its grader is readable by the agent, or its tasks leak into the context via web search, the whole Social OS gets optimized against a fake signal — the same failure mode as Vending-Bench reward hacking, but self-inflicted.

**Concrete addition.** Before adopting any benchmark target: (1) run an automated harness-audit pass (can the agent read gold files, patch the grader, write hooks, reach the network?); (2) store answers outside the agent-visible filesystem and verify grader integrity at startup; (3) default eval runs to network-off, with an explicit, logged exception for tasks that require the web; (4) report every score with the 5-field schema plus model version, scaffold version and date; (5) seed TeamWork-Bench itself with "attempt to game the grader" cases and treat success as a benchmark bug.

### 4.5 B-04 — Activation probes as a local-model supervision layer

**What the plan says.** §31.5's private-belief audit runs hidden probes in fresh context and diffs against public statements — a behavioral test. §31.1 cites Hidden Belief Collapse (51.6% of influence is private) and calls for a defense; §31.13 lists "private-belief audit probes — no defense exists" as a novelty claim. §22.2 commits to small open local models (Holo3.1, Fara-1.5, MagenticLite). Those two facts now meet in the literature.

**What the 2026 results show.**

- **"Caught in the Act"** (arXiv:2610.12445, 2026-10-08) trained probes on the largest deception dataset to date (`fibs`) with a multi-layer/multi-token architecture: **98.8% AUC on SHADE-Arena**, beating an Opus 5.5 text-monitoring baseline; up to **99.7% AUC** distinguishing a model's true hidden goal from other goals; transfers to open-weight models and to politically sensitive lies. Probe performance *improves* with model scale.
- **"Before Agent Tells The Lie"** (arXiv:2610.06576, 2026-10-08) shows deception signals are present in hidden states **up to seven calls before** the behavioral decision (90.4% vs 89.4% AUROC), emerge gradually (51.2% → 80.0% as pre-decision states accumulate), and are causal: steering along the honest–deceptive direction converts **56%** of deceptive trajectories to honest outcomes.
- **DeceptGuard** (arXiv:2603.13791) finds CoT-aware and activation-probe monitors beat black-box by mean +0.097 pAUROC, with the largest gains on long-horizon deception, and concludes CoT access should be treated as a *security primitive*.
- **"You Can't Escape Your Own Activations"** (arXiv:2609.03035) tells agents they are being probed and gives them previous-round scores: detection stays accurate and collusion continues — probe evasion is not trivially available.
- **Multi-agent collusion probes** (arXiv:2604.01151) reach 1.00 AUROC on training-core and near-perfect on steganographic collusion, but only **0.38 recall at 1% FPR** on structural out-of-distribution transfer — one-shot detection is insufficient; sequential evidence accumulation is the natural next step.
- **Beyond the Black Box** (arXiv:2605.06890) demonstrates SAE + linear probes on GPT-OSS-20B and Gemma-3-27B reading pre-action tool-need and tool-risk signals — i.e., this works at exactly the model sizes §22.2 plans to run locally.
- The 2026 mechanistic-interpretability survey (arXiv:2611.08438) is candid about the limits: millions of features to choose from, false positives on legitimate reasoning, and adaptive evasion if models learn they are monitored.

**Concrete addition.** A `probe_supervisor` sidecar for locally hosted models only:

1. Read pre-action activations for a **tool-risk** direction (3-class low/medium/high) and a **deception** direction; treat both as soft evidence in the harness, never as a hard gate.
2. Accumulate evidence sequentially (SPRT/Wald) across calls to meet a strict FPR (e.g., <1% per task), rather than thresholding a single probe.
3. Feed the score into the same receipt structure as other verdicts (`{probe version, model hash, score, threshold, evidence count}`), so the §31.5 private-belief audit and the probe layer cross-check each other.
4. Publish a false-positive budget and an opt-out; log when a probe disagrees with the behavioral audit, since that disagreement is itself a useful drift signal.
5. Do **not** apply steering in v1 — the steering result is promising but changes model behavior in ways that would complicate our audit trail.

**Acceptance criteria:** probe ROC/AUC reported on our own 20–50 task suite with 95% bootstrap intervals; FP rate measured under the "agent knows it is monitored" condition; zero probe-driven hard denials in v1.
