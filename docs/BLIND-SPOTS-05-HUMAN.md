# Blind-Spot Report 05 — Human Factors & Ethics

**Research subagent mandate:** find human-factors/ethics gaps *not* already covered by the approval/UX/human-problems work in `PLAN.md` and `USE-CASES-EXPANSION-V2.md`.
**Date:** 2026-11-17 · **Evidence window:** 2025–2026 (with a few foundational 2021–2024 HITL/trust studies where they are the canonical source).

---

## 1. Executive summary

The docs are unusually strong on **mechanism**: the permission pipeline (§6), the threat model (§24), the demographics atlas (§29), and the multi-agent social layer (§31) already specify tiers, confirm prompts, receipts, and a "capability retention" aspiration. What they largely lack is the **human-factors science of how a person actually calibrates to, oversees, and lives with a powerful local agent**.

The single most important finding: the docs treat **human oversight as a free good**. §6.3, §24.5, and §31.9 assume that adding a confirm button produces meaningful human control. The research says the opposite — keeping a human "in the loop" reliably *increases* willingness to use automation while *decreasing* decision accuracy (PNAS 2024), and "clinician presence alone does not make oversight meaningful" (Nature 2026). Combined with the docs' own cited fatigue numbers (13.6% human refusal vs 89% auto-mode block, §24.4), this means the current design risks shipping **rubber-stamp oversight**: a compliance prop that launders agent errors through a human click.

The second cluster of gaps is **the harness's own UI as a product surface**: §26.3's "a11y-first" is about the *agent reading other apps' accessibility trees*, and §2.10 of the use-case doc is about the *agent acting as a prosthetic for disabled users*. Neither makes the harness's own TUI/tray/overlay WCAG/ADA-conformant. Likewise, the docs cite children's stats and one California law (§29.2, §2.6) but miss the 2026 regulatory step-change: the **EU KIDS Act (COM(2026) 681, Sept 17 2026)** with mandatory zero-knowledge age verification and AI-chatbot rules.

Deliverable below: **15 substantive blind spots**, each with evidence, why it matters, a concrete design addition, and a priority. Prior-art check first so nothing duplicates §29's demographics.

## 4. Top 5 in depth

### 4.1 Human-in-the-loop is not a safety property — trust calibration must be earned (gaps #1, #2)

The docs' most load-bearing assumption is that a human confirmation is a meaningful control. The evidence contradicts it. The PNAS experiment (2024) found that retaining human oversight **increased uptake of automated decision support but decreased the quality of final decisions** — people preferred the system more while monitoring it worse. EDPS (Sept 2025) names the "flawed assumption" directly: supervising humans do not stand outside the automation; their judgment is shaped by it. And the 2026 medical-AI paper concludes clinician presence alone is insufficient for meaningful oversight.

This compounds the docs' own numbers: §24.4 reports 13.6% human refusal vs 89% auto-mode block. If a human approves ~86% of the time, "human-in-the-loop" is close to "human-as-rubber-stamp." Trust calibration research (Springer 2025; Nature 2024; 30-year review 2025) shows both over-trust (automation bias, worse on unfamiliar/objective tasks — exactly a powerful general agent's domain) and under-trust (algorithm aversion after one salient failure) are predictable.

**Design consequence.** Stop counting confirms as safety. Introduce two mechanisms: (a) a **Meaningful Oversight Test** in the rule engine — a rule routes to a human only if error cost is high *and* the confirm card lets the reviewer independently judge correctness; otherwise a deterministic guard handles it. (b) **Reliability Calibration telemetry** — measure acceptance of correct vs incorrect agent proposals, show per-action confidence and known-failure tags, and probe periodically. This converts "we have approvals" into "our approvals catch errors."

### 4.2 Consent must become a lifecycle, not a button (gap #6)

§6.2's resolution chain and §29.11's tier constitution are excellent *taxonomies*, but the 2026 agentic-AI literature is explicit that they are insufficient as *architecture*. The ScienceDirect 2026 paper proposes separating data access, algorithmic inference, synthetic interaction, and delegated action into distinct governance layers — a close match for a harness that reads files, calls models, and mutates the OS. The JMIR 2026 health-data paper operationalizes dynamic consent as three stages: administration → decision → enforcement. Curity's guidance says agent consent must be **granular, time-limited, and revocable**.

**Design consequence.** A **Consent Ledger** co-equal with the audit log: each grant carries scope, TTL, delegation depth, and a revocation path; just-in-time grants for one-off needs; signed consent receipts; and a single "what have I allowed?" view. This also underpins the elder/caregiver (#13) and household (#14) work — those are *consent-boundary* problems, not new permission rules.

### 4.3 The harness's own UI is an accessibility obligation (gap #10)

There is a dangerous conflation in the docs: §26.3 "a11y-first" means the agent reads *other applications'* accessibility trees; USE-CASES §2.10 means the agent serves disabled users. Neither says the harness's **own** TUI, tray, confirm cards, task tree, or desktop overlay conform to WCAG 2.2 AA. With EAA enforcement live since mid-2025 and ADA Title II momentum, that is a compliance gap and a product contradiction.

The prior art is concrete: desktop WCAG audits require real screen-reader testing and keyboard-complete flows; the Community Access project (2026) already publishes a WCAG 2.2 AA conformance workflow for AI coding tools. Neurodivergent UX extends this: predictable confirmation cadence, low-stimulation mode, literal language, no countdown pressure — note §2.10's "hyperfocus interruption contract" is a *use case*, not a property of our own interruptions.

**Design consequence.** Make WCAG 2.2 AA a ship gate for every user-facing surface; add keyboard-only confirm paths, screen-reader state announcements, non-color status coding, and a Low-Stimulation Mode. This is cheap now and expensive retrofit later.

### 4.4 Child safety crossed a regulatory threshold in Sept 2026 (gap #12)

The docs cite CA SB 243 and kids' usage stats. Since then the EU KIDS Act (COM(2026) 681, published 17 Sept 2026, feedback closes 3 Dec 2026) converts non-binding DSA guidance into feature-by-feature prohibitions: minimum age 15, **zero-knowledge age verification**, AI-chatbot memory off by default for minors, bans on relationship-simulating dependency design, and OS duties to pass age signals. COPPA amendments became enforceable 22 Apr 2026; the UK Children's Code and DSA Art. 28 set the baseline.

A local desktop agent with OS-level reach is squarely in scope ("operating systems would have a limited duty to pass on age signals").

**Design consequence.** Build an **Age-Signal Abstraction Layer** now: accept a ZK adult/minor attestation (store no DOB), auto-apply a minor-safe profile when minor or unknown, and keep a jurisdiction-aware compliance matrix. Because the harness is local-first, a conservative "unknown = minor-safe" default costs little and de-risks a fast-moving regime.

### 4.5 Measure fatigue or it will measure you (gaps #8, #9)

§24.4 and §28.2 declare "fatigue-aware approval design" but never define a metric — and the 2026 clinical alert-fatigue systematic review found measurement methods are "not clearly or consistently defined" even in mature fields. Without instrumentation, the claim is unfalsifiable and the "fewer cards" philosophy is guesswork.

Two research threads give us the instruments. First, **fatigue KPIs** (approval acceptance rate, median time-to-approve, rubber-stamp rate for <1s approvals, trend) with automatic batching when thresholds trip. Second, an **ambient channel** backed by evidence: Sidekick (arXiv 2026, 30 participants) showed ambient color/state cues improved multitasking with computer-use agents versus chat or peripheral text. Third, **takeover latency**: L3 driving research gives ~7s as a safe handover budget and shows warning modality/lead time govern correct intervention. A background agent should have an explicit Takeover Time Budget, a graduated handover request, and a pre-staged context packet.

**Design consequence.** Add a 4-state ambient cue channel (idle/progressing/needs-you/stuck) with non-color coding, instrument fatigue and takeover metrics in §13 evals, and treat "time-to-correct-intervention" as a first-class number alongside ASR and pass^k.
